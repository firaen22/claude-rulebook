## Findings

1. **Critical — parent termination destroys the only cleanup authority**

At [v26.sh:218](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-yauch-Documents-claude-code-technique/d8a69179-c2ab-4c5b-927b-3b9417b15cb6/scratchpad/round23/review/sol26/files/v26.sh:218), the parent trap kills only its direct background job—the worker. The probe PID exists only inside that worker at [v26.sh:731](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-yauch-Documents-claude-code-technique/d8a69179-c2ab-4c5b-927b-3b9417b15cb6/scratchpad/round23/review/sol26/files/v26.sh:731). Once the worker is killed, nobody executes the inner deadline or PID kill at line 744. Therefore the “left for up to the 1s budget” statement at lines 700–704 is false. [verified: busy probe wrote its PID before PID-directed TERM; hook exited 0 with zero stdout, but probe remained alive and spinning 1.1 seconds later until explicitly killed]

Concrete fix: flatten ownership so the outer watchdog directly spawns and tracks the probe as well as the eventual worker, killing all known live PIDs before exiting. Alternatively, provide a cleanup supervisor that survives parent termination; merely adding a trap inside the worker is insufficient for SIGKILL/D-state paths. Add a ready-gated `H01 × D_pid_TERM` regression.

2. **Critical — a session-detached probe survives caller group KILL**

The claim at [v26.sh:691](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-yauch-Documents-claude-code-technique/d8a69179-c2ab-4c5b-927b-3b9417b15cb6/scratchpad/round23/review/sol26/files/v26.sh:691) is true only while the worker remains alive to execute its PID kill. If a probe calls `setsid()` and the caller then KILLs the original group, both parent and worker die immediately; the detached probe survives. [verified: `killpg(SIGKILL)` succeeded, hook returned `-9`, detached probe PID remained alive, zero hook stdout]

This also reproduced an EOF hazard: the detached child retained an injected fd 10 pipe writer; its reader returned `WOULD_BLOCK` until the orphan was killed, then returned EOF. [verified: `fstat` plus nonblocking pipe read]

Concrete fix: either use a cleanup supervisor outside the caller-directed group that tracks and kills the probe, or explicitly narrow the contract and cryptographically/structurally guarantee that only trusted non-detaching binaries can run. Under the present “every path” contract, the current topology cannot satisfy this case.

3. **Critical — the inner deadline is not strictly nested inside the outer deadline**

The independent calculations at [v26.sh:653](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-yauch-Documents-claude-code-technique/d8a69179-c2ab-4c5b-927b-3b9417b15cb6/scratchpad/round23/review/sol26/files/v26.sh:653) and [v26.sh:791](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-yauch-Documents-claude-code-technique/d8a69179-c2ab-4c5b-927b-3b9417b15cb6/scratchpad/round23/review/sol26/files/v26.sh:791) can resolve to the same `SECONDS` value if worker scheduling is delayed across an integer boundary. Then the outer loop may kill the worker before the worker kills its probe. [verified: instrumented copy delayed only `_pend` assignment until `SECONDS==1`; on attempt 3 of 3, hook exited 0/zero stdout while the busy probe survived]

Concrete fix: establish one absolute deadline in the cleanup owner before spawning anything, prevent new probe creation once its inner cutoff has passed, and keep the cleanup owner alive until every runnable child is confirmed terminated. Flattening the nested ownership also removes this race.
